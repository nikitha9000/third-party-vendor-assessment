Vendor Risk Scoring Methodology

1\. Purpose



This document defines the methodology used to identify, measure, prioritize, and evaluate third-party vendor security risks.



The methodology supports consistent risk assessment and helps organizations determine appropriate risk treatment actions.

2\. Risk Assessment Approach



The assessment uses three stages:



&#x20;   Inherent Risk Assessment



&#x20;   Security Control Effectiveness Assessment



&#x20;   Residual Risk Assessment



3\. Likelihood Scoring



Likelihood measures the probability of a security risk occurring.

Score	Rating	Description

1	Rare	Very unlikely to occur

2	Unlikely	Could occur under unusual circumstances

3	Possible	May occur occasionally

4	Likely	Expected to occur in some circumstances

5	Almost Certain	Expected to occur frequently

4\. Impact Scoring



Impact measures the potential business consequences of a risk.

Score	Rating	Description

1	Insignificant	Minimal business disruption

2	Minor	Limited operational impact

3	Moderate	Noticeable business disruption

4	Major	Significant financial or operational impact

5	Severe	Critical business disruption or major data exposure

5\. Inherent Risk Calculation



Formula:



Inherent Risk = Likelihood × Impact

Score	Risk Rating

1–4	Low

5–9	Medium

10–16	High

17–25	Critical



Example:



Risk: Unauthorized access to confidential business data.



Likelihood = 4



Impact = 5



Inherent Risk = 4 × 5 = 20 (Critical).

6\. Vendor Questionnaire Scoring



Each questionnaire response receives a score.

Response	Score

Yes	2

Partial	1

No	0

Pending	Not scored



Maximum possible score = 50 points.



Formula:



Control Compliance Percentage = (Total Obtained Score / Maximum Applicable Score) × 100



Assessment interpretation:

Percentage	Result

90–100%	Strong

75–89%	Satisfactory

50–74%	Needs Improvement

Below 50%	High Concern



Only completed and applicable questions should be included in the denominator. Pending questions must not be treated as zero.

7\. Security Control Effectiveness



Control effectiveness evaluates whether existing security controls sufficiently reduce identified risks.

Rating	Score	Description

Effective	3	Control is implemented and supported by sufficient evidence

Partially Effective	2	Control is implemented but has identified gaps

Ineffective	1	Control is absent or does not adequately address the risk

Not Assessed	N/A	Insufficient information to evaluate



Evidence must be reviewed before assigning a control effectiveness rating.

8\. Residual Risk Assessment



Residual risk represents the remaining risk after considering implemented security controls.



For this project, use the following illustrative calculation:



Residual Risk = Inherent Risk × (1 − Control Effectiveness Factor)



Control effectiveness factors:

Control Effectiveness	Factor

Effective	0.80

Partially Effective	0.50

Ineffective	0.00



Example:



Inherent Risk = 20



Control Effectiveness = Partially Effective



Residual Risk = 20 × (1 − 0.50)



Residual Risk = 10 (High).



The factors are illustrative project assumptions, not official ISO or NIST formulas. Residual scores should be rounded to the nearest whole number before assigning the risk rating.

9\. Risk Treatment Options

Treatment	Description	Example

Mitigate	Reduce likelihood or impact	Require stronger authentication

Avoid	Stop the risky activity	Discontinue use of an unsuitable vendor

Transfer	Allocate risk through contractual or insurance arrangements	Contractual liability provisions

Accept	Formally acknowledge remaining risk	Management-approved risk acceptance

10\. Risk Acceptance Criteria

Residual Risk	Recommended Action

Low	Accept with routine monitoring

Medium	Management review and periodic monitoring

High	Remediation plan and management approval

Critical	Immediate escalation and formal risk treatment decision



Risk acceptance must be approved by an authorized business or risk owner.
